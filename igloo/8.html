Ireland 2036

The real island, rendered live. Terrain and sea-floor depth from satellite survey, satellite imagery draped over it, real building outlines raised to their real heights across Dublin, and every place - the Cliffs of Moher, the Liffey, Arklow Bank - at its true longitude and latitude. Floor and walls are one continuous view, so the ground runs out from under you and away to a horizon at eye level. The flight is slow, level and always forward, so the room stays comfortable. Then a wave of light sweeps out from Dublin, and Ireland 2036 takes shape around you.

Elevation and bathymetry: Terrain Tiles on AWS (SRTM, NED, GEBCO). Imagery: Esri World Imagery. Buildings: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, via Overpass - real footprints at real heights where a height or storey count is recorded, and at a height inferred from the building type where it is not. Place, route and offshore-site coordinates: public geographic data. Towns outside the surveyed boxes are massed, not modelled. The 2036 structures are the only buildings here that do not exist, and they are the ones that rise with the sweep. Swap any service at the top of the script if the venue holds a licensed source.

Room controls

If Dublin looks sandy in the last act, lower Grade warmth and City light at night. Cloud cover scales the whole show's weather; the deck still thins and thickens through the three minutes.

Margin masks a projector that overshoots the floor. Keystone corrects an off-axis mount. O rotates the world on the floor. Blend sets how far up the wall a scene's footage dissolves into the live world; the world itself is always continuous across the joint.

The pad moves through one chord per act. Chimes mark each caption and the map's heartbeat.
